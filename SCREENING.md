# Screening — The Funniest Office Joke

Judge brief criteria, in order:
1. One-line joke about office life, real joke with setup + turn.
2. Turns on something anyone who has worked in an office knows.
3. Surprises, turn the reader does not see coming, not a plain complaint.
4. Lands in one line, every word earning its place, punchline last.
5. Feels new, not a joke already going round the office.

Scores are 1–5 per criterion, in the brief's order.

## Candidates

### 1. "Our printer only jams when the deadline is real, which is how I learned it reads the calendar."
- 1: 5 — setup (printer jams at deadlines) + turn (it reads the calendar).
- 2: 5 — the deadline printer jam is universal office trauma.
- 3: 5 — the printer having intent is a turn nobody sees coming.
- 4: 5 — one line, punch word "calendar" last, nothing wasted.
- 5: 4 — printer jokes exist, but a printer that reads the calendar is a fresh frame.
- Total: 24

### 2. "I scheduled a meeting to discuss why we have too many meetings, and it ran over."
- 1: 5 — setup + turn, the meeting itself becomes the problem.
- 2: 5 — everyone has sat in the meeting about meetings.
- 3: 4 — the overrun is ironic but partly telegraphed by the setup.
- 4: 5 — tight, punch word "over" last.
- 5: 3 — "meeting about meetings" is a known trope.
- Total: 22

### 3. "I replied-all to say stop replying-all, and now 47 people know I'm the problem."

- 4: 4 — "47 people" earns its place; "the problem" is a soft punch word.

### 4. "The meeting that could have been an email had a follow-up meeting to discuss the email."

- 3: 3 — the twist is logical, almost expected once the setup lands.

### 5. "HR called my burnout a growth opportunity and my paycheck a learning experience."

## Winner

**Candidate 1: "Our printer only jams when the deadline is real, which is how I learned it reads the calendar."**

Highest total (24/25), top marks on the first four criteria in order, and the only line whose turn (the printer reads the calendar) is both a genuine surprise and a fresh frame on the most universal office pain in the brief.

## Runner-up

**Candidate 2: "I scheduled a meeting to discuss why we have too many meetings, and it ran over."**

Loses on criteria 3 and 5: once the setup names a meeting about meetings, the overrun is half-expected, and the "meeting about meetings" trope is already going round every office. The printer line keeps its turn hidden until the last word.
